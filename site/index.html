Security model

An identity model built for your security review.

Agents act inside a person's rights, for one run. Every step lands in an audit chain that your auditors verify with their own key.

One token, two names

sub is the person. act.sub is the agent. Every request and every audit record names both.

Rights end with the run

A delegated token lives one hour at most and expires when its run finishes. Runs get member rights, also when an administrator starts them.

Admin rights need two opt-ins

One from the organization and one from the person. When the organization switch turns off, every personal opt-in resets.

Alert text stays data

The agent reads alert and tool content as escaped, untrusted data in its own block, apart from its task.

See the delegated tokenRFC 8693 
{
  "typ":   "obo",
  "sub":   "user:ana",                // the person
  "act":   { "sub": "agent:coder" },  // the agent
  "run":   "run_8f2c",                // bound to one run
  "scope": "READ WRITE",              // member rights
  "exp":   "+1h"                      // or when the run ends
}

Evidence your auditors can check.

  • The whole flow, recordedAlert intake, every authorization decision, token events, policy changes, tool calls and agent runs, for people and agents.
  • A chain your auditors verifyEach record links to the one before it with HMAC-SHA256. Auditors run audit-verify on an export with their own key.
  • Identifiers onlyRecords hold actors, scopes and IDs. Secrets, prompts and incident text stay out of the stream.
  • Mapped to ISO/IEC 27001:2022Evidence for A.5.15 to A.5.18, A.8.2 privileged access, A.8.15, A.8.16 and A.5.24 to A.5.28. Straight to Sentinel, Loki, Splunk, Elastic or any HTTP collector.

Budget-aware agents

Agents respect your AI budget.

When your AI gateway reports that the budget is used up (HTTP 429), the agent pauses for the cooldown the gateway states, then continues with new work. Your LLM backend gets one call per pause. Alerts open incidents and page people during the pause, as always.

From first alert to final fix

One platform for the full incident lifecycle.

Alerts, paging, investigation, response and learning share one timeline. People and agents write to the same record.

The next level gets the page.

Alerts come in from Alertmanager or any webhook. Anyone in the organization can raise an alert for any team.

  • Severe alerts open an incident and link to it.
  • Escalation policies page the person on call, then the next level after the time to acknowledge.
  • Administrators set limits for all teams, for example "the next level after 15 minutes at most".
  • Pages go to your chat, SMS or push service through one signed webhook.

Agents start before you do.

A configured agent analyzes the alert with your tools: metrics, logs, deploys and tickets through MCP. Its findings are on the timeline when you open the page.

  • Automatic analysis on alert, or one button: Analyze, Create patch, Remediate.
  • The timeline fills itself from agent findings and updates.
  • Each agent run links to its OpenTelemetry trace: model turns, tool calls, tokens and errors.

The right people, with the right rights.

Teams come from your identity provider. Schedules, overrides and escalation live next to the incident. Agents act on behalf of the person who starts them.

  • A colleague covers a shift with one click, for a set period.
  • Claude, coding agents and your own bots connect through the built-in MCP server, webhooks or gRPC.
  • Live updates on every open browser.

Less writing after the incident.

Tell Mayday how you solved it: type it, write it with a stylus or dictate it. Agents turn it into a runbook and draft the postmortem from the timeline.

  • Runbooks in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.
  • Policies that teams can make stricter, for example "postmortem mandatory from SEV2".
  • Reports: MTTA, MTTR and postmortem compliance per team and period.

Questions

Answers for your review.

How do we evaluate Mayday?

Request an evaluation. In one afternoon our engineers install Mayday in your cluster and connect your identity provider, alert sources, AI gateway and SIEM. Your team sees it work with your own identities, alerts and audit trail.

What does your team prepare?

A Kubernetes namespace or a VM, a PostgreSQL database, an OIDC client, one alert source, your AI gateway URL and your SIEM endpoint.

Where does our data go?

To your PostgreSQL and the AI gateway and tools you configure. Traces go to the OpenTelemetry endpoint you set. The audit stream goes to your SIEM.

Can an agent get admin rights?

Run tokens carry member rights. Admin rights through an agent need the agent's own role, or token exchange with both the organization switch and the person's own opt-in on.

What happens when the LLM or its budget is unavailable?

Alerts open incidents and page people at once. On HTTP 429 the agent pauses for the stated cooldown, then continues with new work.

How do our alert sources and pagers connect?

Alert sources post to the Alertmanager endpoint or the generic alerts API (POST /api/v1/alerts). Pages go to your chat, SMS or push bridge through one signed webhook. Teams come from your identity provider.

Will it scale to our organization?

The design target is 50,000 to 100,000 concurrent users and 10,000 incidents and alerts a day. With PostgreSQL it runs many stateless replicas.

Which identity providers work?

Entra ID, Okta, Keycloak, or any OIDC and SCIM 2.0 provider. Groups become teams and owners.

Which agents can connect?

Claude, coding agents and your own bots, through the built-in MCP server, signed webhooks or gRPC. Writes by agents are configurable.
